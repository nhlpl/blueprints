The rocket uses **radiolysis of water** – gamma rays or high‑energy particles split water into hydrogen and oxygen. The hydrogen is used as propellant in a biological thruster, while the oxygen supports metabolism. The radiation source is either **ambient space radiation** (solar protons, GCR) or a **radioactive core** (e.g., Po‑210) embedded in the rocket.

The thruster is a **muscular nozzle** made of **contractile actin‑myosin fibers** (evolved from smooth muscle) that pulse at a frequency determined by the golden ratio. Each pulse ejects a burst of hydrogen gas, producing thrust.

### **4. Propulsion System**

**Reaction**:  
\[
2H_2O \xrightarrow{\gamma} 2H_2 + O_2
\]  
Hydrogen is stored in a flexible bladder; oxygen is used for cellular respiration (energy for the muscles).

**Thruster design**:
- **Nozzle**: Contractile muscle fibers arranged in a golden‑angle spiral. When stimulated by the ant nervous system, they contract rhythmically.
- **Pulse frequency**: \(f = 6.18\) Hz (golden ratio / 10). Each pulse ejects 1 g of H₂ at \(v_e = 3.82\) km/s (exhaust velocity – achieved by magnetic field from the quantum chip).
- **Thrust per pulse**: \(F = \dot{m} v_e = 0.001 \times 3820 = 3.82\) N.
- **Pulse rate**: 6.18 Hz → average thrust = \(3.82 \times 6.18 \approx 23.6\) N.
- **Total impulse**: For a 100 s burn, total Δv = \(F \cdot t / m = 23.6 \times 100 / 618 \approx 3.82\) km/s – enough for LEO.

**Specific impulse**:  
\[
I_{sp} = \frac{v_e}{g} = \frac{3820}{9.81} \approx 389 \text{ s}
\]  
Comparable to chemical rockets, but the propellant (water) is renewable via radiolysis.
